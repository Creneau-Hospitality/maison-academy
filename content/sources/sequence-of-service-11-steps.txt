Sequence of service steps
1. Welcome
The welcome is the first point of communication between the unit and the
guest. Using positive behaviours with open body language, great eye
contact and by welcoming with a warm and genuine smile. The guest must
be made to feel as though they are genuinely welcome in a fashion unlike
any other previous dining or drinking experience. They should feel as though
their custom is important and appreciated.
You as a server should introduce yourself to the guest as soon as possible as
follows: make eye contact with the host as you are approaching the table. If
the guests are talking, wait a few seconds to attract their attention.
Do not wait for a guest to greet and approach you first. Remember that a
guest will wait happily for a lot longer once he knows you have noticed him.
Service should be formal, it should be interactive and enjoyable. The waiters
should be efficient and friendly in their approach, to make the guests feel
comfortable. Treat your regular customers with respect but make them feel
relaxed and called them by their name.
2. Taking the drink order
Direct yourself to the entire table. Make eye contact and smile, stand
straight. Make sure you have a pen and pad and that the pen is working.
Take out your scratch pad. Smiling, excuse yourself and ask, “Would you like
to order a drink?
If the guests look undecided, make a few suggestions.
Immediately after taking the drink order, offer water, “What type of water
would you like? We offer still water like … or … sparkling water?
Write down all orders. Always show confidence and good knowledge of the
product.
Introduce the order into Micros, making sure you double check the table
number, number of guests and that the order has been correctly introduced
with all the special request.
3. Serving drinks
Ensure that your hands are clean and dry before handling glassware. Ensure
that there are no fingerprints on the glass or lipstick color (marks) for hygiene
reasons. Pick up the glass by the base or stem and not by the rim.
DO NOT RUN!!! Walk slowly giving priority to guests who are moving around
the restaurant. Direct yourself to the table holding firmly the beverage tray.
Serve the drinks ladies and older gentlemen first as indicated above.
Announce the drinks during serving them from the right side
When you have served everyone, make a step backwards and say, “Enjoy
your drinks!”
4. Taking the food order
Again make sure that you have a pen working properly. Ensure visibility by not
standing behind any guest. Make eye contact and smile. Take out your
Order pad. Smiling, ask with a clear voice, “May I take your order?”
Make sure to collect all the needed information from your guests like allergies,
dietary requirements etc.
Be confident and explain the dish so the guests have an idea and know what
they are ordering. Once the order was taken, repeat back to each guest with
an eyecontact.
Like this you are avoiding any possible misunderstanding.
Take the order as per procedure described above. Stand straight and nod
your head after every order taken and say, “Thank you.”
NOTE: The Waiter must always be 100% sure before leaving the table that
he/she fully understood the order and that it was recorded correct. Repeating
the order to the guests is a must and will avoid mistakes.
5. Serving food
Double check the food items against the captain order. Make sure all plates
are clean from sauce drips or fingerprints, and check that you have all the
corresponding condiments.
Make your way to your table maintain a straight posture and a warm smile.
Approach the guest form the right side. If the guest does not see you say,
“May I?”
Make sure you aren’t creating any discomfort to your guests. Hold your plates
straight; ensure the sauce doesn’t spill. Present every plate and describe it in
few words.
Serve the sauces or condiments and explain to the guest what they are
(name, ingredients, and spiciness).
NEVER CARRY MORE THAN 3 PLATES AT THE TIME.
Once finished serving, direct yourself to the table and say, “Enjoy your
APPETIZERS, MAINS...”
6. Monitor the meal and check on guest satisfaction
Quite simply this means you as a professional should care that the guest is
enjoying their meal or beverage. Waiter must visit each table at least once
during the course of the meal to check on guest satisfaction. The most
adequate time to do it 3-5 minutes after they got their meal.
Make eye contact with the host as you are approaching the table. If the
guests are talking wait a few seconds to attract their attention.
The question should be personalised to the guest i.e: “How is your Steak?”
rather than just “How is your meal?” This makes a world of difference to the
guest, who feels as though you have an interest in them and their
experience–as a professional you should. If the dish is not satisfactory find out
why and, if necessary get it changed
Remember! A guest who complains is giving you as second chance – unlike
the other guests who do not say anything and never return to your restaurant.
Therefore, take advantage of the opportunity and do whatever it takes to
recover the guest.
7. Calling the main course
Monitor the meal and estimate the speed at which the guests are eating. Go
to the service station and send Mains away.
8. Clearing plates
When clearing the table, organize the cutlery in such a way that the handle
of the fork is facing towards your thumb. To prevent the fork from moving
around the plate, place your thumb on top of the handle. Slide the knife
underneath the fork to prevent it from traveling around the plate. Make sure
not to bump into the head of the guest, and avoid dropping cutlery or
leftovers.
9. Presenting the dessert menu
Pick up one dessert menu per person and direct yourself to the table. Hand
out the menus as previously described.
Since a lot of our guests might not be familiar with the restaurant, and our
specialties, you need to be able to do some “suggestive selling”. At the same
time ask if the guest would like some coffee, tea or digestives.
10. Presenting the check
The check should be presented no longer than 2 minutes after it has been
requested. Check on the screen that everything has been posted and print
the check.
Return to the table and make eye contact with the host. Wait until the guest
has left the cash, credit card.
Thank the guest and proceed with the payment.
11. Departing the guest
When you see the guest standing up, approach the table thank them, get
their feedback and even if you are performing a task, remember to pause
briefly to help and bid farewell.
Check on top of the table, on the chairs and below the table to ensure that
the guests did not forget anything.
Smile and say bid farewell, “Thank You, and goodbye, hope to see you
again”.
Remember a warm, genuine farewell is essential.
The last experience for the guest is often the only one they remember, so
always say “good-bye”, “thank you” and “come again” in a warm, sincere
